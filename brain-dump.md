Everyone now agrees that perceptual restoration is misaligned with downstream understanding — that's stated explicitly in ICML 2026 and in the Visual Quality Paradox paper. But the two leading responses go in opposite directions: Robust-U1 says reconstruct better (internally, with RL), Robust-R1 says don't reconstruct, reason about the degradation instead.

- Nobody is asking whether the information survived. Every method above is about producing a better input or a more tolerant model. None of them establishes whether the evidence needed to answer a particular question is present in the measurement at all. Robust-U1 hits a wall precisely where information is "extensively lost" but has no way to detect that boundary; Seeing is Believing detects it only via supervised annotation in one narrow domain. That's an inverse-problems question, and inverse-problems people have the tools for it — which is exactly why your grant fits.

- The gap is finding out a procedure to figure out if the actual information survives using the restoration techniques that are being used.
