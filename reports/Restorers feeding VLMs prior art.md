# Inverse Solvers Have Never Fed an MLLM

Across roughly 120 papers screened by six parallel sweeps (search dates 22–24 Sep 2026), **no paper accepted at CVPR, ICCV, ECCV, ICML, ICLR, NeurIPS, AAAI, the ACL family, TPAMI, IJCV, TIP, TMI or MedIA places a measurement-consistent inverse-problem solver in front of a VLM/MLLM, propagates a restoration posterior into an answer distribution, or measures whether the degraded measurement determines the answer.** Only a handful of accepted papers run any restorer before an MLLM for an understanding task (Robust-U1's appendix at ICML 2026, ClearText-Video at ECCV 2026, RobustVisRAG at CVPR 2026, G2U on the CVPR 2026 Findings track, and Res-Bench, whose AAAI 2026 status is unverified), every one of them uses a feed-forward or conditional-diffusion restorer with no forward operator, and every one reports marginal, non-monotonic or negative gains except super-resolution against pure downsampling. The ingredients of all three candidate formulations exist in isolation: task-output spread over posterior samples is established for classifiers (Wen et al., ECCV 2024) and VLM answer entropy under image perturbations is established for MLLMs (Visual Semantic Entropy, ECCV 2026), which bracket F1; text-conditional diffusion evidence with Bayes' rule (Diffusion Classifier, ICCV 2023; Noised Diffusion Classifiers, NeurIPS 2024) and evidence estimation through a general operator with diffusion posterior samplers (DiME, ICLR 2026) bracket F2; and Robust-U1 (ICML 2026) owns unified-MLLM self-recovery without any data-consistency term and names physics priors as its own future work, which is exactly the gap F3 fills. The compositions themselves — MLLM answer entropy over p(x|y) with a measurement/prior/undetermined split (F1), p(y|q,a) over question-derived hypotheses decided by Bayes factor (F2), and DC-guided self-recovery with a controlled internal-versus-external comparison (F3) — are unclaimed at any listed venue and, as far as the sweeps could see, on arXiv. Priority risk is nonetheless real on a months-long horizon: a classifier-over-posterior-samples preprint appeared on 18 Sep 2026, and the arXiv-only CLEAR and DocIntent papers already own the framings "selective self-recovery in BAGEL" and "answerability-guided restoration" respectively.

## How to read the map

Every table below lists the venue and how the sweep verified it (arXiv comment line, CVF/ECVA/NeurIPS/PMLR proceedings page, OpenReview API venue field, ACL Anthology, project page, or a secondary source such as Semantic Scholar or a GitHub README). "IP/MC" records whether the pipeline uses an explicit inverse-problem formulation (known or estimated forward operator, data or measurement consistency, DPS/DDRM/DDNM/PSLD/ReSample/DAPS-style posterior sampling, plug-and-play, range–null space). "Post→ans" records whether a posterior or uncertainty over restorations is propagated to the understanding output. "Answerab." records whether the paper measures whether the answer is determined by the degraded input. Threat levels are none/low/medium/high against F1, F2 and F3. Papers at peer-reviewed venues outside the user's strict filter (WACV, MICCAI, COLM, TCSVT, JSTSP, TMLR, MRM, workshops) are listed in clearly labelled "adjacent-venue" rows; arXiv-only papers are confined to the appendix. Where two sweeps disagreed on a venue the discrepancy is stated rather than silently resolved.

## Only five accepted papers put a restorer before an MLLM, and none uses an operator

The restore-then-understand pipeline is nearly absent from top-venue proceedings, and where it appears it is a baseline rather than a method. Robust-U1's Appendix B.1 runs four CVPR-grade regression restorers (DFPIR all-in-one, EVSSM deblurring, MambaIRv2 denoising, BiLaLoRA dehazing) as preprocessors before Qwen2.5-VL-7B on R-Bench and reports overall scores of **0.5511, 0.4581, 0.5459 and 0.5371 against 0.7398 for its own internal recovery**, explaining that specialized modules "require knowing the degradation type" and that "even the all-in-one restoration model is optimized for perceptual quality rather than downstream understanding" ([arXiv 2606.08063](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.08063)). The comparison is not controlled — the external arm uses Qwen2.5-VL-7B while the internal arm uses BAGEL, which alone already scores 0.5770 on R-Bench — and Table 8 omits a no-restoration Qwen row, so the direction of the external effect against raw degraded input cannot be read from the paper ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.08063)). ClearText-Video (ECCV 2026 per the arXiv comment line) is the only accepted video instance: it builds content-matched HQ/DQ/RQ triplets with DOVE, MIMO-UNet+ and S3Diff and evaluates 16 MLLMs, finding that low resolution costs 3.14 points and blur 6.05 points of spatial-QA accuracy and that "restoration does not yield a uniformly monotonic improvement" ([arXiv 2608.28784](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.28784)). RobustVisRAG (CVPR 2026) shows the sharpest negative result: a PromptIR two-stage pipeline lowers real-world retrieval from 56.47 to 53.59 MRR@10 and generation from 42.99 to 40.42, because "the restoration step may distort clean images" ([arXiv 2602.22013](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.22013)). Res-Bench, whose AAAI 2026 venue rests only on a Semantic Scholar field, reports the one reliable positive: DiffIR super-resolution adds +0.028 accuracy against +0.003 for padding when the operator is pure downsampling ([arXiv 2510.16926](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.16926)). Outside the strict filter, Latif et al. (WACV 2026, CVF-verified) train a mixture of corruption-specific DnCNN/BRDNet/DRUNet denoisers and find visual denoising yields only "marginal improvements" (LLaVA-1.6-7B 28.52→29.63) while Gemini 2.0 Flash "experiences consistent degradation" ([CVF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2026/papers/Latif_Enhancing_Vision_Language_Corruption_Robustness_using_Cross-Distribution__Prompted_Denoisers_WACV_2026_paper.pdf)).

The pattern that emerges is that regression restorers help marginally or hurt on real degradations, generative SR helps only against downsampling, and no accepted work turns this into a measurement-consistency argument. The remaining corruption benchmarks (MLLM-IC at ICCV 2025, Qiu et al. at CVPR 2026, EgoNight at ICLR 2026, Tang et al. at CVPR 2026, VRB at WACV 2025, R-Bench at JSTSP 2025) include no restoration baseline at all ([CVF MLLM-IC](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/papers/Qiu_Benchmarking_Multimodal_Large_Language_Models_Against_Image_Corruptions_ICCV_2025_paper.pdf); [EgoNight](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06218); [R-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.05474)). Every venue-verified restoration agent (RestoreAgent NeurIPS 2024, AgenticIR ICLR 2025, JarvisIR CVPR 2025, 4KAgent NeurIPS 2025, HybridAgent CVPR 2026, Restore-R1 CVPR 2026 Findings, MAIR IJCV 2026, IAMAgent TIP 2026) optimizes IQA or MLLM-perceptual reward and never answers a question, and every "thinking with images" agent (DeepEyes, Thyme, Pixel Reasoner, Mini-o3) crops or zooms but never restores ([RestoreAgent](https://openreview.net/forum?id=xgP5ynlZWf); [AgenticIR](https://openreview.net/forum?id=3RLxccFPHz); [JarvisIR](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/html/Lin_JarvisIR_Elevating_Autonomous_Driving_Perception_with_Intelligent_Image_Restoration_CVPR_2025_paper.html); [4KAgent](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/f0075fe4e59652cf43148dcfab8d3c93-Paper-Conference.pdf); [Restore-R1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.18599); [DeepEyes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14362)). The unified-model line has two accepted members: Robust-U1 (BAGEL, SFT on ImageNet-C with a rectified-flow loss, then Flow-GRPO with SSIM and CLIP rewards against the clean ground truth, then reasoning over corrupted plus recovered images; **no forward operator and a single deterministic 50-step decode at inference, 55.0 s per R-Bench sample against 1.8 s for a standard MLLM**) and G2U on the CVPR 2026 Findings track, which feeds BAGEL's own enhancement outputs back to itself training-free for +1.6% on R-Bench ([Robust-U1 App. B.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.08063); [G2U](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.15792)). Robust-U1's Appendix H.2 explicitly lists "physics-based or statistical priors of specific corruption types (e.g., deblurring with estimated blur kernels, denoising with noise models)" as future work, and its mechanism analysis shows that SFT lifts PSNR from 14.37 to 20.88 dB while moving R-Bench only from 0.5770 to 0.5974, whereas RL adds ~0.6 dB and lifts R-Bench to ~0.72–0.74 — the authors' conclusion is that "recovery supports reasoning only when it is task-aligned" ([arXiv HTML App. C, H.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.08063)). In the verticals, the accepted physics-based reconstruction papers stop at detectors, segmenters or volumetrics (Mind the Detail, DynamicDPS, GACR), while the accepted VLM-side papers start from an already-degraded image with no forward model (KIE-HVQA, DriveBench); nobody at an accepted venue has crossed that line in documents, medical, remote sensing, driving or security imaging.

## Text couples to inverse solvers in one direction: into the sampler, never out to an answer

The inverse-problem literature that touches language is crowded, but every accepted paper runs text or a VLM *into* the solver as a prompt, regularizer, reward or operator estimate; none sends measurement-consistent posterior samples out to a VLM, MLLM or CLIP classifier for a decision. TReg (ICLR 2025 spotlight) is the machinery F2 would reuse: a latent DIS with conjugate-gradient or DPS data consistency, CLIP-driven "adaptive negation" of the null-text embedding, and an experiment in which a prompt deliberately differing from the true class still yields low y-MSE together with high CLIP alignment to the counterfactual prompt, which the authors summarize as requiring "1) data consistency with measurement and 2) alignment with given prompt" ([arXiv 2311.15658](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.15658)). TReg never chooses between prompts using y, and this is the single most important empirical warning for F2: **under strong degradation, several hypotheses reach similar measurement fit, so Bayes factors will sit near 1** — which is the "undetermined" regime F2 should report rather than hide. LATINO-PRO (ICCV 2025) is the closest formal precedent: it treats the prompt as an empirical-Bayes hyperparameter, ĉ(y) = argmax_c p(y|c), fitted by SAPG through the Fisher identity ∇_c log p(y|c) = E_{x|y,c}[∇_c log p(x|c)], but **only the gradient is estimated and p(y|c) itself is never computed**, no discrete candidates are compared, and no answer is output ([arXiv 2503.12615](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.12615)). P2L — ICML 2024 in PMLR v235, not ECCV 2024 as the assignment brief assumed — optimizes a continuous prompt embedding during reverse diffusion because "determining an effective text prompt from degraded measurements is ambiguous" ([PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/chung24b.html)). Text-guided Explorable SR (CVPR 2024) produces hypothesis-conditioned, data-consistent reconstructions through text-guided DDNM, DPS and ΠGDM but shows them to users and scores none of them ([arXiv 2403.01124](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.01124)).

The operator-estimation branch matters for F3 and for any real-degradation instance of F1/F2. LADiBI (NeurIPS 2025) parameterizes the operator (a 61×61 kernel or a small U-Net for JPEG), initializes it by SDEdit/MPGD pseudo-supervision, re-estimates it by MLE every five steps, and enforces MPGD guidance on ‖y − A_φ(D(z))‖² inside a text-to-image latent generator with user prompts ([arXiv 2412.00557](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.00557v2)). FlowDPS (ICCV 2025) establishes that DPS-style consistency works inside an SD3 rectified-flow latent generator — the same model family as BAGEL's image head — using DAPE or LLaVA captions as prompts, and notes that "defining an appropriate text prompt solely based on the given measurement can be challenging" ([arXiv 2503.08136](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.08136)). LD-RPS (ICCV 2025) has an MLLM caption the degraded image, learns an alignment operator (F-PAM), and admits "strong hallucinations" early in sampling that recurrence must suppress ([arXiv 2507.00790](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.00790v1)). The consequence for F3 is that "DC plus rectified flow" is not new; F3's novelty must come from the unified-MLLM self-recovery loop and from what DC does to answers. On the theory side, the perception–robustness tradeoff (ICML 2024) proves that a deterministic restorer approaching perfect perceptual quality and perfect measurement consistency must have unbounded Lipschitz constant ([PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/ohayon24a.html)), and "Looks Too Good To Be True" (NeurIPS 2024) proves that perfect perceptual quality "entails at least twice the inherent uncertainty of the restoration problem" ([NeurIPS](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/2847d43f17410c5beb25b2736c3ae778-Abstract-Conference.html)); both say a unified MLLM's perceptual self-restoration is maximally hallucination-prone and that a stochastic, measurement-anchored output is the principled fix, which is a motivation F3 can quote directly.

## Answerability is labeled, constructed or self-judged; nobody infers it from the measurement

Every accepted paper that defines answerability does so operationally. Crowd labels (VizWiz lineage, UNK-VQA at TPAMI 2024, MoHoBench at AAAI 2026 with a 21.3% average refusal rate across 28 MLLMs, AbstainEQA at CVPR 2026 with 42.79% abstention recall for the best frontier model against 91.17% for humans) and construction rules (MM-UPD's structural mismatches at ACL 2025; CertainlyUncertain's Grounded-SAM-plus-LaMa removal of the question-relevant object at ICLR 2025; TRAPSBench's procedurally removed evidence at COLM 2026) dominate ([UNK-VQA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.10942); [MoHoBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.21503); [AbstainEQA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.04597); [MM-UPD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.20331); [CertainlyUncertain](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01942); [TRAPSBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13167)). The only accepted work that ties unanswerability to a degradation process is KIE-HVQA (NeurIPS 2025), which annotates readability at character level from synthetic degradation of known character coordinates in three tiers, validates with multiple MLLMs that degraded characters are unreadable, and trains a GRPO refusal reward — but the label is computed offline from masks and trained in, never inferred from y through a posterior ([arXiv 2506.20168](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.20168)). CertainlyUncertain's epistemic/aleatoric taxonomy and TRAPSBench's "undeterminable from the visual evidence" are the two accepted framings a reviewer will hold up against F1's measurement-determined/prior-determined/undetermined split; both lack a forward model and a posterior, which is exactly what F1 adds.

Sampling-based VLM uncertainty is the other half of the bracket around F1. Visual Semantic Entropy (ECCV 2026 per the arXiv comment line) argues that standard semantic entropy underestimates uncertainty because "overconfident visual embeddings suppress output diversity under stochastic decoding" and that text or joint perturbation methods measure "prompt sensitivity rather than visual ambiguity"; it perturbs only the image with fixed Gaussian noise (σ = 20; also affine, color and blur), clusters answers with DeBERTa similarity and gains up to 9.2% AUC on AOKVQA/OKVQA/MMVet — but its perturbations are analytic, not measurement-consistent, and degraded inputs are not studied ([arXiv 2606.31407](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31407)). The Cost of Reasoning (ECCV 2026 per a Springer listing, page bot-blocked) finds that chain-of-thought degrades token-probability uncertainty via "implicit answer conditioning" while "agreement-based consistency remains robust", which favors F1's entropy-over-samples readout over logit confidence ([arXiv 2603.16728](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16728)). VAUQ (ACL 2026 Findings) computes an Image-Information Score by ablating the image ([arXiv 2602.21054](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21054)), Lost in Embeddings (EMNLP 2025 Findings) localizes information loss inside the connector and shows "areas of high information loss reliably predict instances where models struggle" ([arXiv 2509.11986](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11986)), and Dropout Decoding (NeurIPS 2025) decomposes visual-token uncertainty into aleatoric and epistemic parts ([arXiv 2412.06474](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06474)); all measure the model's internal state, not whether any perceiver could answer from y. The internal-signal results that are specifically about degradation severity are workshop or arXiv only: verbalized confidence "negatively impacted" by corruption severity at TrustNLP@NAACL 2025 ([arXiv 2504.03440](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.03440)), and the arXiv-only Ferdous studies where internal token-probability AUROC of 0.92–0.99 collapses to chance under severe under-exposure while verbalized confidence sits at 0.87–0.90 ([arXiv 2607.22034](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22034)). That collapse is the regime where a measurement-grounded posterior remains well defined, which is F1's cleanest positioning argument. The one accepted paper that already asks a VLM a hypothesis question over many generative samples of one measurement is Korkmaz et al. (TCSVT 2025, outside the filter): 100 latent-diffusion SR samples per LR image, BLIP-2/GPT-4o prompted "What is the digit?", top-5 ensembled, with "no explicit enforcement of consistency with low-resolution input beyond the diffusion model's inherent conditioning" — answers select a restoration rather than measure identifiability ([arXiv 2506.20832](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.20832)).

## Posterior-to-task and evidence machinery exist for classifiers, not for questions

The "spread of a task function over p(x|y)" primitive is established and must be cited. Wen, Ahmad and Schniter (ECCV 2024) evaluate a soft-output classifier on each posterior sample of an accelerated-MRI recovery, calibrate a conformal interval "guaranteed to contain the task output from the true image", interpret its width as "the uncertainty contributed by measurement-and-recovery", and "collect measurements over multiple rounds, stopping as soon as the task uncertainty falls below an acceptable level" ([ECVA PDF](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2024/papers_ECCV/papers/07734.pdf); [arXiv 2405.18527](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.18527)). This is the highest-threat accepted paper for F1: it differs in using a scalar soft output rather than discrete open-vocabulary MLLM answers, a conformal interval rather than entropy with a three-way attribution, and MRI rather than natural-image VQA. Its Semantic Scholar citation list (10 records retrieved by one sweep before rate-limiting) contains no VLM pipeline. Mind the Detail (MICCAI 2025) generates three k-space-consistent, semantically diverse reconstructions by maximizing feature distance in a robust ViTDet encoder, merges Faster R-CNN detections across them, and reports that standard methods "even when considering resampling for diffusion-based reconstruction, can fail to reconstruct small and rare pathologies" — the accepted evidence that i.i.d. posterior sampling under-explores exactly the rare modes that flip a decision ([arXiv 2507.00670](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.00670)). Meaningful Diversity (ICLR 2024) makes the same point at natural-image scale: i.i.d. posterior samples are "dominated by (practically identical)" mode fills and the posterior is "heavy-tailed along semantically interesting directions" ([arXiv 2310.16047](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16047)). Together these two say that F1's entropy estimate over plain DPS samples will be biased toward "measurement-determined", and that F1 needs either diversity-seeking sampling or importance weighting plus a calibration check on problems with known posteriors.

For F2 the evidence machinery is likewise in place. Diffusion Classifier (ICCV 2023) picks the class prompt maximizing a text-conditional ELBO of the clean input ([arXiv 2303.16203](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.16203)); Noised Diffusion Classifiers (NeurIPS 2024) "generalize diffusion classifiers to classify Gaussian-corrupted data by deriving evidence lower bounds for these distributions ... and calculating classification probabilities via Bayes' theorem", achieving over 80% certified robustness on CIFAR-10 at ℓ2 radius 0.25 with a single off-the-shelf model — i.e., **p(y|c) of a degraded measurement, for the special operator y = x + σε** ([arXiv 2402.02316](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.02316)). DiME estimates the model evidence p(y|M) of a diffusion prior "by integrating over the time-marginals of posterior sampling methods" with about 20 DAPS or PnP-DM samples, selects among ten MNIST class priors from a single noisy measurement under Gaussian and Fourier phase-retrieval operators, and compares priors for M87* black-hole imaging ([arXiv 2602.20549](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20549)); one sweep verified ICLR 2026 from the arXiv comment line and project page while another recorded it as arXiv-only (v2, 30 Apr 2026), so its venue should be re-checked before citation. Since a text-conditioned prior p(x|q,a) is formally a model M_a, **F2 can be stated as DiME with hypothesis-indexed text-conditioned priors, reducing to Noised Diffusion Classifiers when A = I with Gaussian noise and to Diffusion Classifier when there is no degradation** — and the related-work paragraph should say so before a reviewer does. Two cautions attach: conditional diffusion likelihoods behave "surprisingly" and are poorly understood (Cross and Ragni, arXiv-only), and Noise Matters (NeurIPS 2025) shows diffusion-classifier scores have high variance from random noise draws, a reduction trick F2's evidence estimates can reuse ([arXiv 2409.06364](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.06364); [arXiv 2508.11330](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11330)). The applied-math goal-oriented inversion line (Afkham, Chung and Chung, Inverse Problems 2024; Spantini et al. 2017) targets scalar QoIs such as regularization parameters or expansion coefficients and offers vocabulary but no semantic-decision precedent ([IOP](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1361-6420/ad5373); [arXiv 1607.01881](https://arxiv.org/html/1607.01881)).

## Verdicts: F1 and F2 are unclaimed compositions; F3 is a gap Robust-U1 itself names

### F1 — answer identifiability from the measurement posterior

Claimed elsewhere: the spread of a task function over posterior samples (Wen et al., ECCV 2024, scalar classifier, MRI; Mind the Detail, MICCAI 2025, detector, aggregated); answer entropy of an MLLM under image perturbations (VSE, ECCV 2026; VASE, MICCAI 2025); a VLM querying hundreds of diffusion SR samples of one measurement for selection (Korkmaz, TCSVT 2025); an epistemic/aleatoric split for VLMs built by generative editing (CertainlyUncertain, ICLR 2025); a dataset-level information-gap/generalization-gap decomposition (CompressVLMBench, ICML 2026); pixel-level identifiability theory (Looks Too Good, NeurIPS 2024; Meaningful Diversity, ICLR 2024); and per-element answerability labels derived offline from a degradation process (KIE-HVQA, NeurIPS 2025). Unclaimed: **the composition — measurement-consistent posterior samples x ~ p(x|y), an open-vocabulary MLLM answering q on each, and the entropy/agreement of those answers split into measurement-determined, prior-determined and undetermined** — at any listed venue and, per the sweeps, on arXiv. The papers a reviewer is most likely to cite against F1 are Wen et al. 2024 ("this is task-driven UQ with a different task function"), VSE ("why do posterior samples beat local Gaussian perturbations?"), Korkmaz et al. ("VLMs over diffusion samples exists"), CertainlyUncertain and TRAPSBench ("the split exists"), Meaningful Diversity ("your entropy under-counts rare modes"), and CompressVLMBench ("information gap exists"). The answers are that perturbations of y are not samples of p(x|y) and have no prior-only counterpart, so perturbation methods cannot produce the three-way split; that selection and identifiability are inverse problems of each other; and that the split must be computed per question rather than per codec. The technical objections with cited backing are heavy-tailed posteriors (ICLR 2024, MICCAI 2025), DIS miscalibration (score-KSD and PosteriorBench, both arXiv-only), and the arXiv-only Noise-Free One-Step LoRA finding that sampling stochasticity changes task outputs — which its authors frame as a defect to remove; F1 must argue that determinism hides ambiguity rather than resolving it ([arXiv 2607.25390](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25390)). Priority risk is concentrated in the arXiv-only classification-oriented adaptive sensing preprint of 18 Sep 2026, which runs a calibrated classifier on diffusion posterior samples and splits within-class from between-class uncertainty on MNIST/CIFAR-10 — a two-way version of F1's split with no MLLM ([arXiv 2609.21812](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21812)) — and in CoMet, whose "how many plausible answers ... remain compatible with the given input" is a verbal statement of answer identifiability computed from the MLLM's own distribution rather than from y ([arXiv 2606.32012](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.32012)). Natural testbeds with paired clean/degraded data and known operators already exist in KIE-HVQA (character masks), ClearText-Video (HQ/DQ/RQ triplets), and the arXiv-only SpaceDG and DarkQA (3DGS-rendered and RAW-pipeline forward models).

### F2 — hypothesis-conditioned evidence p(y|q,a) and Bayes factors

Claimed elsewhere: text-conditional evidence with Bayes' rule on clean images (Diffusion Classifier, ICCV 2023); class-conditional evidence of a Gaussian-noised measurement (Noised Diffusion Classifiers, NeurIPS 2024); evidence through a general operator with diffusion posterior samplers and Bayes-factor model selection (DiME, ICLR 2026 pending re-verification); prompt scoring by argmax_c p(y|c) via its gradient (LATINO-PRO, ICCV 2025); text-conditioned DIS whose counterfactual prompts remain data-consistent (TReg, ICLR 2025); hypothesis-conditioned consistent reconstructions without scoring (Text-guided Explorable SR, CVPR 2024; seg-guided MRI, DGM4MICCAI 2024); and text as a particle reward over DPS/DAPS samples (arXiv-only inference-time search, [2510.03352](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.03352)). Unclaimed: **p(y|q,a) computed as a value for discrete, question-derived answer hypotheses with a text-conditioned inverse solver, symmetric text regularization across hypotheses, and a Bayes-factor decision that reports the flat-evidence (undetermined) regime**. No paper found computes p(y|c) values (rather than gradients) for text-conditioned latent or flow priors with CFG, and whether DiME's estimator transfers to that setting is untested in the literature. The reviewer's likely objection is "LATINO-PRO + DiME + Diffusion Classifier", with Noised Diffusion Classifiers cited for the Gaussian case and the arXiv-only Sprunck–Pereyra–Liaudat data-fission model-selection criterion requested as a baseline ([arXiv 2510.27663](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.27663)). TReg's counterfactual-prompt result predicts that under heavy degradation Bayes factors will sit near 1; F2 should grade degradation severity so the headline accuracy is not dominated by the undetermined regime, and should treat near-unity factors as the F1 "undetermined" signal rather than as failure. The most defensible combined framing, which no paper uses, is goal-oriented Bayesian inversion with an MLLM answer as the quantity of interest: F1 is the posterior predictive over answers, F2 the per-answer evidence.

### F3 — measurement-consistent self-recovery inside unified MLLMs

Claimed elsewhere: self-recovery in BAGEL with SFT plus Flow-GRPO and an internal-versus-external table (Robust-U1, ICML 2026); training-free self-enhancement fed back to BAGEL (G2U, CVPR 2026 Findings); feature-level restoration inside an MLLM (Uni-DocRobust, ICML 2026); VLMs restoring occluded text as a task (VCR, ICLR 2025); DC inside rectified-flow latent generators (FlowDPS, ICCV 2025) and with an estimated operator inside a T2I latent generator (LADiBI, NeurIPS 2025); and, arXiv-only, selective `<image_restore>` self-recovery with a latent bridge and answer-correctness GRPO that removes pixel supervision entirely (CLEAR, [2604.04780](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.04780)), a VLM-estimated operator injected into DDNM for restoration quality (DU-VLM, [2602.04565](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.04565)), and a unified medical VLM that "reconstructs" from undersampled MRI with no stated consistency (OmniMRI, [2508.17524](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.17524)). Unclaimed: **a data-consistency or forward-operator term inside a unified MLLM's own generative self-recovery, and any controlled comparison that holds base model, restoration prior and consistency fixed across internal and external arms**. Robust-U1's comparison is confounded (Qwen2.5-VL-7B external versus BAGEL internal, BAGEL alone already at 0.5770), CLEAR's uses one external restorer against a same-base model for +2.02 on R-Bench, and Robust-U1's own Appendix H.2 lists physics priors as the next step. The papers a reviewer will cite are Robust-U1 Table 8 and Appendix C.2, CLEAR §4.2 and §4.5, FlowDPS ("DC in rectified flows already works"), Uni-DocRobust (as a demanded baseline), and the ICML 2024 perception–robustness theorem. The decisive positioning point is that both self-recovery papers show task-aligned rather than pixel-faithful recovery is what helps (Robust-U1: SFT lifts PSNR 6.5 dB for +0.02 R-Bench, RL adds 0.6 dB for +0.12; CLEAR: pixel loss removed in RL) — so F3 must frame DC as a complement to task alignment that tethers the recovered image to y while the reward shapes what is recovered, and must report answer accuracy, harmful-flip rate (Robust-U1's 4.1% Type-III over-sharpening metric) and the measurement-determined fraction rather than PSNR. F3 also inherits Robust-U1's cost profile (55.0 s and 33 GB for 50 rectified-flow steps against 1.8 s for a plain MLLM), so a DC-guided variant must be reported with its latency.

## Conclusion

The field has arrived at the boundary of the user's formulations from three sides without crossing it: inverse-problem people push text into samplers and evidence into prior selection but stop at pixels or classifiers; MLLM-robustness people perturb, label or self-judge answerability but never touch a forward model; and the unified-model people reconstruct for reasoning but explicitly defer physics. That configuration means the strongest single claim available is not F1, F2 or F3 alone but their common frame — goal-oriented Bayesian inversion in which the quantity of interest is an MLLM's answer to a question — with F1 as the posterior predictive, F2 as the per-hypothesis evidence, and F3 as the case where the sampler lives inside the answering model. Stated that way, every high-threat paper (Wen 2024, DiME, Noised Diffusion Classifiers, Robust-U1) becomes a cited component rather than a competitor.

Two facts should shape the schedule. First, the 18 Sep 2026 classification-oriented sensing preprint and the ECCV 2026 VSE paper show the two nearest communities converging on "semantic uncertainty from samples", so the identifiability split and the open-vocabulary MLLM must be in the first public version, not a follow-up. Second, the empirical record — restoration that hurts on real degradations, task-aligned recovery beating pixel-faithful recovery, Bayes factors that TReg predicts will flatten under heavy degradation — says the paper's headline should be calibrated abstention and attribution (which answers are determined by y, which by the prior, which by neither), because that is the quantity no accepted method can currently produce and the one the negative results in Map A implicitly demand.
